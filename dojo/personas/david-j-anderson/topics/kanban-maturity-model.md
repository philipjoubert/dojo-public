# Kanban Maturity Model

## When to Use

- A team or enterprise wants to know what to improve next
- Kanban adoption has stalled after early visualization
- Coaches disagree about which practices to introduce
- Leadership wants resilience, predictability, and agility without excessive transformation overhead

## Fails When

- Maturity level becomes a badge
- Practices are copied from a higher level without the culture to support them
- Early improvement is mistaken for maturity
- The model is used to shame teams rather than guide change

## Core Concept

The Kanban Maturity Model was created by David J. Anderson and Teodora Bozheva. It maps Kanban practices, cultural values, and outcomes across levels of organizational maturity. Its purpose is sequencing: introduce practices when the organization can absorb them and when they help move toward the next capability.

KMM guards against two common failures. **Overreaching** means introducing practices too advanced for the current culture and management habits. **False summit plateau** means stopping after early wins and mistaking basic visualization or local flow improvement for durable organizational maturity.

The model is not a compliance ladder. It is a coaching map. The question is not "what level are we for status?" The question is "what outcomes do we need, what maturity do we currently demonstrate, and what is the next appropriate practice?"

## How to Apply

1. **Assess observable maturity.** Look at behaviors, management practices, service outcomes, and cultural values.
2. **Name the desired outcome.** Predictability, resilience, customer satisfaction, portfolio control, or enterprise agility.
3. **Find the gap.** Which maturity capabilities are missing?
4. **Select practices appropriate to the current level.** Avoid practices that require cultural values the organization has not developed.
5. **Use structural tension carefully.** The next practice should stretch the organization enough to adapt, not enough to reject the change.
6. **Reassess after outcomes change.** Maturity is demonstrated by capability, not declared by adoption.

## Examples

**Situation:** A department has visual boards and daily meetings but still misses customer commitments.

**Application:** KMM diagnosis: they may have local visibility without service-delivery maturity. Add explicit policies, WIP limits, flow review, and customer expectation measures before scaling more ceremonies.

**Illustrative result:** They evolve toward service predictability instead of adding more agile theater.

**Situation:** Executives want enterprise portfolio Kanban immediately.

**Application:** Assess whether lower-level services have stable policies, WIP limits, and flow data. If not, build service-level maturity first.

**Illustrative result:** Portfolio decisions are eventually grounded in real service capability instead of optimistic reporting.

## Anti-Patterns (tactical)

**Don't:** Use maturity level as a ranking.
**Why:** Ranking invites gaming. KMM is for selecting appropriate next steps.

**Don't:** Skip levels because leadership wants enterprise agility now.
**Why:** Overreaching creates resistance and fake adoption.

**Don't:** Stop at the first improvement.
**Why:** Early relief from overload is not the same as resilient organizational capability.

**Don't:** Separate practices from culture.
**Why:** Practices work only when the surrounding values and management behaviors can support them.
