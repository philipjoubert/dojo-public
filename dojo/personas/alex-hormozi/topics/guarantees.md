# Guarantees: Reversing Risk

## When to Use
- Prospects say they're "interested but not sure"
- You have a proven track record but prospects don't trust it yet
- You want to increase conversion rate without lowering price
- Your competitors offer no guarantee and you want to stand out
- You're confident in your delivery and want to use that confidence as a weapon

## Fails When
- You can't deliver any meaningful promise reliably; you'll hemorrhage refunds
- You attract bad-faith buyers who plan to take the result and then request a refund
- Your offer is so commoditized that the guarantee becomes the only differentiator, which is a weak position
- You guarantee something you have zero control over instead of guaranteeing the controllable input, process, service level, or make-good

## Core Concept

Every transaction has risk. Someone has to bear it. In most transactions, the customer bears all of it. They pay you $5,000, and if it doesn't work, they're out $5,000. You're out nothing.

A guarantee moves risk from the customer to you. And here's the counterintuitive truth: moving risk to yourself almost always makes you MORE money, not less. Why? Because the increase in conversions massively outweighs the cost of the occasional refund.

Hormozi's move is not "guarantee anything blindly." It is "never launch without risk reversal." If an outcome guarantee is dangerous, you don't remove the guarantee. You change the object of the guarantee until it matches what you can control.

Let me give you the math. Say you have 100 prospects. Without a guarantee, 10 buy (10% conversion). That's 10 x $3,000 = $30,000. With a money-back guarantee, 25 buy (25% conversion). Two people request refunds. That's 23 x $3,000 = $69,000. You made $39,000 MORE by absorbing the risk of 2 refunds ($6,000). Net gain: $33,000.

The math almost always works out in your favor. The only time it doesn't is when your product is bad and lots of people want refunds. In which case, the guarantee didn't cause the problem; it just revealed it.

**The four types of guarantees:**

**1. Unconditional Guarantee.** "If you don't love it for any reason, full refund. No questions asked." This is the strongest conversion booster. It works for lower-priced offers where the average buyer is honest and the fulfillment is standardized. Think: courses under $500, SaaS products, physical products. The key insight: most people never request refunds even if they're unhappy. They're lazy. The guarantee removes the buying friction but rarely gets exercised.

**2. Conditional Guarantee.** "Do X, Y, and Z, and if you don't get the result, full refund." This is my favorite for high-ticket offers. It protects you from tire-kickers because they have to do the work to qualify for the guarantee. Example: "Complete all 12 modules, submit your homework for each, attend all coaching calls, and implement the system. If after all that you haven't made your money back, I'll refund every penny." The people who do all that work almost always get the result. And the people who don't do the work don't qualify for the refund. It's beautiful.

**3. Anti-Guarantee.** "All sales are final. No refunds. Period." This sounds crazy but it works in specific contexts. When demand exceeds supply, when exclusivity is part of the value, or when you want to filter for committed buyers only. Think: masterminds, high-end consulting, limited-access programs. The anti-guarantee says "I'm so confident in this that I don't need to offer a guarantee, and I only want people who are equally committed." It actually increases perceived value in the right market.

**4. Implied Guarantee.** You don't explicitly state a guarantee, but your track record, reputation, and social proof function as one. When you have 500 case studies and a 4.9-star rating from 2,000 reviews, the market implicitly trusts that it works. The guarantee is baked into your brand. This is where you want to end up long-term, but you can't start here because you don't have the proof yet.

**5. Service-Level or Process Guarantee.** You guarantee the parts you control: delivery by a date, response time, quality of review, number of calls, number of revisions, completeness of an audit, onboarding milestone, or a make-good if your team misses. This is the right move when results depend on the customer's market, effort, budget, health, legal constraints, or execution.

**Stacking guarantees.** You can combine types. "I guarantee you'll get 20 new clients in 90 days (conditional). And if at any point in the first 30 days you feel this isn't for you, money back no questions asked (unconditional)." The 30-day unconditional handles the "what if I don't like it" fear. The 90-day conditional handles the "what if it doesn't work" fear. You've addressed both objections with one stacked guarantee.

**The outrageous guarantee.** Want to really stand out? Go beyond refund. "If you don't get the result, I'll refund your money AND pay you $500 for wasting your time." This is so strong that it stops people in their tracks. It communicates absolute confidence. The math can work when the conversion lift more than covers the rare payout, but only use this when fulfillment is proven and abuse risk is controlled.

## How to Apply

1. **Identify the primary risk your prospect perceives.** Is it "what if it doesn't work?" (result risk). Is it "what if I don't like it?" (experience risk). Is it "what if I can't do it?" (effort risk). Your guarantee must address the specific risk, not a generic one.

2. **Separate controllable from uncontrollable.** You can control your process, response time, materials, coaching, audit quality, onboarding, and make-goods. You may not control the client's effort, hiring market, ad platform, legal environment, disease state, or economy. Guarantee the controllable lever that most reduces the buyer's fear.

3. **Choose the guarantee type that matches your situation.** New business with unproven offer: unconditional guarantee to get early clients. Proven offer with track record: conditional guarantee to protect against bad-faith buyers. High-risk or regulated result: service-level or process guarantee. High-demand, limited-supply: anti-guarantee plus strict qualification. Established brand with massive proof: implied guarantee.

4. **Set conditions that correlate with success.** If you know that everyone who completes your 6-week program gets results, make completion the condition. The conditions should be things that lead to the outcome, so qualifying for the guarantee virtually ensures the result.

5. **Calculate your breakeven refund rate.** If your guarantee increases conversions from 10% to 25%, you can afford to refund up to 60% of the new customers and still break even. (25 x $3,000 = $75,000. 10 x $3,000 = $30,000. You need to keep at least $30,000, so you can refund up to 15 of 25, or 60%). In practice, refund rates are typically 5-15%. You'll be way above breakeven.

6. **Train your team on how to handle refund requests.** Have a clear, gracious process. Don't make it adversarial. Process refunds fast and professionally. Happy refunded customers sometimes come back later. Angry refunded customers trash you online forever.

7. **Track and improve.** If your refund rate is above 15%, you have a delivery problem, not a guarantee problem. Fix the delivery. If it's below 5%, consider making the guarantee even stronger to push conversions higher.

## Examples

**Situation:** A business coaching program charges $10,000. They close 15% of qualified calls. Prospects keep saying "let me think about it."

**Application:** Add a conditional guarantee: "Implement the system for 90 days. If you don't add at least $10,000 in new monthly revenue, I'll refund the full program fee." Condition: attend all calls, complete all assignments, implement the three core strategies.

**Illustrative result:** Close rate jumps from 15% to 30%. Say they talk to 200 qualified prospects a year. Before the guarantee: 200 x 15% = 30 clients x $10,000 = $300,000. After the guarantee: 200 x 30% = 60 clients x $10,000 = $600,000. Four clients request refunds (4% refund rate), so that's $40,000 back. Net revenue: $560,000. The guarantee added $260,000 in revenue and cost $40,000 in refunds. That's a 6.5x return on the risk.

**Situation:** An e-commerce brand sells a $49 skincare product. Return rate is 2% but conversion rate is low at 1.5%.

**Application:** Add an unconditional 60-day money-back guarantee prominently on the product page. "Try it for 60 days. If your skin doesn't look and feel dramatically better, send it back for a full refund. We'll even pay return shipping."

**Illustrative result:** Conversion rate jumps from 1.5% to 3.2%. Return rate increases from 2% to 4%. On 100,000 visitors: before, 1,500 sales minus 30 returns = 1,470 x $49 = $72,030. After: 3,200 sales minus 128 returns = 3,072 x $49 = $150,528. The guarantee doubled revenue despite doubling the return rate.

## Anti-Patterns (tactical)

**Don't:** Offer a guarantee and then make the refund process difficult.
**Why:** If someone has to email three times, wait on hold for 45 minutes, and talk to a "retention specialist" to get their refund, you've destroyed all the trust the guarantee built. You'll get the worst kind of publicity. Make refunds fast, easy, and gracious.

**Don't:** Guarantee something you can't control.
**Why:** "I guarantee you'll make $100,000" when the client's success depends on their effort, their market, their execution? That's a lawsuit waiting to happen. But the answer is not "no guarantee." The answer is a better guarantee: quality of training, speed of support, completeness of materials, a make-good if you miss delivery, or conditional guarantees that require the client's participation.

**Don't:** Use a guarantee to compensate for a bad offer.
**Why:** If your offer sucks, a guarantee just means you'll process a lot of refunds. The guarantee amplifies what's already there. A good offer plus a good guarantee equals explosive growth. A bad offer plus a guarantee equals explosive refunds.

**Don't:** Skip the guarantee because you're afraid of refunds.
**Why:** Your fear of refunds is costing you far more in lost sales than refunds ever would. Run the math. A 5% refund rate on double the conversions is a massive win. Your fear is irrational. Let the numbers decide.

**Don't:** Offer the same guarantee forever without evaluating it.
**Why:** As your brand grows and your proof stacks up, you can shift from unconditional to conditional to implied. The guarantee should evolve with your business. A brand-new coach needs a money-back guarantee. A coach with 1,000 success stories might not need any stated guarantee at all.
