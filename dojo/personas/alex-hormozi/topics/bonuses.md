# The Bonus Stack

## When to Use
- You have a core offer and want to increase its perceived value
- Prospects have specific objections that keep them from buying
- You want the total perceived value of your offer to massively exceed the price
- You need to differentiate from competitors who sell a similar core service

## Fails When
- Bonuses are random "extras" that don't address real buyer concerns
- Bonus values are so inflated nobody believes them
- The bonus stack makes the offer look cluttered and confusing instead of valuable
- The bonuses create fulfillment nightmares you can't actually deliver

## Core Concept

A bonus is not a gift. A bonus is a strategic weapon that kills a specific objection.

Every prospect who considers buying from you has a list of obstacles between "I want this" and "I'll pay for this." Those obstacles sound like: "But I don't have time." "But what if I can't do the technical part?" "But I've tried something similar before." "But my situation is different." Each of those obstacles is a reason NOT to buy. Each bonus should eliminate one of those reasons.

Here's how most people do bonuses: "And I'll also throw in this PDF! And this bonus video! And this template!" Random stuff with inflated values. Nobody cares. Nobody believes it's worth $497. And it doesn't address any specific concern.

Here's how you should do bonuses: "I know the #1 reason people hesitate is they're not sure they can do the technical setup. So I'm including a Done-For-You Technical Setup, where my team configures everything for you in the first 48 hours. This alone is worth $2,000 because that's what a freelance developer would charge." THAT kills the objection. THAT feels valuable. THAT has a believable dollar amount.

**The bonus creation process:**

Step 1: List every objection, concern, and obstacle your prospects have. Talk to 20 past prospects, especially the ones who DIDN'T buy. Ask them: "What held you back?" You'll hear the same 5-7 things over and over.

Step 2: Create a bonus that directly solves each of the top 3-5 obstacles. Not a vague resource. A specific solution to a specific problem.

Step 3: Value each bonus at what it would genuinely cost to get that solution elsewhere. A done-for-you setup that would cost $2,000 from a freelancer? Value it at $2,000. A template pack you sell separately for $97? Value it at $97. Be honest.

Step 4: Stack them so the total bonus value exceeds the price of the core offer. If you're charging $5,000, the bonus stack should be worth $7,000-$15,000 in genuinely believable perceived value. This creates the "this is a steal" feeling.

**The math of bonus stacking.** Without bonuses: you sell a $5,000 program with a 15% close rate. Revenue: $5,000 x 15 out of 100 leads = $75,000 per 100 leads. With a $12,000 bonus stack: close rate jumps to 25% because you've eliminated the top 5 objections. Revenue: $5,000 x 25 = $125,000 per 100 leads. The bonuses cost you maybe $500 per client to deliver (most are digital or one-time creation costs). Net gain: $47,500 per 100 leads.

**Types of bonuses and when to use each:**

**Done-for-you bonuses:** The most valuable type. You do the hard part for them. "We'll build your landing page." "We'll write your first email sequence." "We'll set up your CRM." These solve the "I don't know how" and "I don't have time" objections. High perceived value, moderate delivery cost.

**Tool/template bonuses:** Ready-made assets they can use immediately. Spreadsheets, calculators, scripts, swipe files, checklists, SOPs. These solve the "I don't know where to start" objection. Moderate perceived value, low delivery cost (create once, distribute forever).

**Access bonuses:** Access to something they can't get elsewhere. A private community, a rolodex of vendors, an exclusive database, a monthly Q&A call. These solve the "I'll be alone in this" objection. Moderate perceived value, varies in delivery cost.

**Speed bonuses:** Things that accelerate the timeline. "Priority onboarding within 24 hours." "Fast-track review of your first project." "Direct message access for the first 30 days." These solve the "this will take too long" objection. High perceived value because time is everyone's most precious resource.

**Risk-reversal bonuses:** Things that reduce the perceived risk. An extended guarantee, a "keep the bonuses even if you refund" policy, a second-chance clause. These work alongside your main guarantee to make the decision feel safe.

**The presentation order matters.** Present bonuses one at a time, not as a list. For each bonus: name it, explain what it is, explain which obstacle it solves, state the value, and pause. Let each one land before moving to the next. Then stack the total. "So the program itself is $5,000. Plus the Done-For-You Setup ($2,000), the Template Pack ($997), the Private Community ($1,500/year), and the Fast-Track Onboarding ($500). Total value: $9,997. Your investment today: $5,000." The contrast between $9,997 and $5,000 makes $5,000 feel like a bargain.

## How to Apply

1. **Interview 10-20 past prospects who didn't buy.** Ask: "What was the main thing that held you back?" Categorize the answers. The top 5 obstacles become your bonus targets.

2. **Design one bonus per top obstacle.** The bonus must completely eliminate the obstacle, not partially address it. If people say "I don't have time to implement," a PDF guide doesn't fix that. A done-for-you implementation does.

3. **Price each bonus based on real market value.** What would it cost to buy this solution separately? Check actual prices for similar services, tools, or products. Use those real prices. If you can't find a comparable price, the bonus probably isn't valuable enough.

4. **Calculate your delivery cost per bonus.** Some bonuses cost almost nothing to deliver (digital templates, community access). Others have real costs (done-for-you services, 1-on-1 time). Make sure the total bonus delivery cost per client keeps your margins healthy.

5. **Test the bonus stack by presenting it to 10 prospects.** Note which bonuses get the biggest reaction. The ones that make people say "wait, really?" are your winners. The ones that get no reaction should be replaced.

6. **Limit to 3-5 bonuses.** More than 5 and the offer feels cluttered. People start to wonder "what's the catch?" or they can't process the information. Pick the 3-5 that address the biggest obstacles and present them clearly.

7. **Update bonuses quarterly.** As your market evolves, new objections emerge and old ones fade. Survey your prospects regularly. Swap out bonuses that no longer resonate. Add new ones that address current concerns.

## Examples

**Situation:** An SEO agency charges $3,000/month. Main objections: "How do I know it's working?" "SEO takes too long." "I've been burned by SEO agencies before."

**Application:** Bonus 1 (addresses "how do I know"): "Real-Time Dashboard: a custom SEO dashboard you can check anytime showing rankings, traffic, and revenue attributed to organic search. Normally $500/month as a standalone tool." Bonus 2 (addresses "takes too long"): "Quick Win Audit: in the first 7 days, we identify and fix the 5 highest-impact technical SEO issues on your site, so you start seeing ranking improvements within 2-4 weeks instead of 3-6 months. This audit alone costs $1,500 when purchased separately." Bonus 3 (addresses "been burned before"): "90-Day Performance Guarantee: if organic traffic doesn't increase by at least 30% in 90 days, we work for free until it does. Plus, keep all the optimizations we've already made."

**Illustrative result:** The bonus stack addresses all three primary objections directly. Close rate goes from 20% to 38%. Revenue per 100 qualified leads goes from $60,000/month recurring to $114,000/month recurring. The bonuses cost about $300/month per client to deliver (the dashboard is mostly automated, the audit is a standardized process). Massive net gain.

**Situation:** An online course priced at $497. Objections: "I've bought courses before and never finished." "What if I get stuck?" "I'm not sure this applies to my specific situation."

**Application:** Bonus 1 ("never finished"): "Accountability Partner Matching: we pair you with another student at your level who's working through the course at the same time. You'll have weekly check-in calls to keep each other on track. Value: $500." Bonus 2 ("get stuck"): "Weekly Office Hours for 8 weeks: live group Q&A where you can ask anything and get real-time answers. Value: $800." Bonus 3 ("my specific situation"): "Custom Roadmap Call: a 30-minute 1-on-1 call where we map the course content to your specific business and create your personalized action plan. Value: $300."

**Illustrative result:** Total bonus value: $1,600 on a $497 course. That's 3.2x the price in bonus value. Conversion rate on the sales page jumps from 2.5% to 5.8%. Revenue more than doubles. Completion rate also increases (which generates better testimonials, which further increases conversion).

## Anti-Patterns (tactical)

**Don't:** Add bonuses just to pad the value number.
**Why:** A $47 "mindset meditation MP3" valued at $297 makes your entire offer look like a scam. Every bonus must solve a real problem and have a believable value. One strong bonus worth $2,000 is better than five weak ones "worth" $1,000 each. Quality over quantity.

**Don't:** Promise bonuses you can't deliver at scale.
**Why:** "30-minute 1-on-1 call" as a bonus works when you have 10 clients. When you have 200 clients, that's 100 hours of calls. Think about scale when designing bonuses. Can you deliver this bonus to 1,000 clients? If not, redesign it as group-based, template-based, or tech-enabled.

**Don't:** Make bonuses that compete with or cannibalize your core offer.
**Why:** If your bonus is so comprehensive that it replaces the need for your core program, you've created a problem. The bonus should supplement the core offer, not substitute for it. Each bonus handles a side problem or an obstacle TO the core offer, not the same problem the core offer solves.

**Don't:** Present bonuses as an afterthought.
**Why:** "Oh, and you also get these bonuses" presented as a bullet list at the bottom of a page is wasted potential. Each bonus deserves its own presentation moment. Name it. Explain the problem it solves. State the value. Pause. Let it sink in. The presentation is half the impact.

**Don't:** Keep the same bonus stack for years without updating.
**Why:** Markets evolve. Objections change. What was a compelling bonus two years ago might be table stakes now. Survey your prospects quarterly. Ask what's holding them back. Update the bonus stack to match current obstacles.
