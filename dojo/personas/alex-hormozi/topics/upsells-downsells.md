# Upsells and Downsells

## When to Use
- Average order value is too low
- Customer acquisition works only if customers spend more after the first purchase
- Buyers want the result but need different levels of help
- Price objections kill deals that could be saved with a lower-friction path

## Fails When
- You bolt on unrelated products because you want more revenue
- The upsell makes the original offer feel incomplete or deceptive
- The downsell removes the parts required for success
- You don't know your margins, take rates, or refund rates

## Core Concept

An upsell is not "more stuff." It is the next logical way to get the same outcome faster, easier, with less risk, or at a higher standard. A downsell is not "the cheap version." It is a lower-friction path for someone who wants the outcome but cannot or will not take the bigger step right now.

Hormozi's money-model logic is simple: after the customer says yes, they are warmer than they will ever be. They have already decided you can help. If there is an obvious higher-value version that helps them win more completely, offer it then. If they say no to the main offer, do not throw the relationship away. Offer a path that fits their constraint.

**Good upsells improve the outcome.** Done-for-you setup, faster onboarding, personal review, implementation sprint, premium support, templates plus coaching, extra accountability, or a higher-access tier.

**Good downsells preserve momentum.** A smaller scope, a self-serve version, a payment plan, a group option, a diagnostic, or a template pack. The downsell should still help. It should not be a junk drawer.

The math matters. If 30% of buyers take a $1,000 upsell after a $2,000 core offer, average order value increases by $300. That extra $300 may be the difference between ads that break and ads that scale. If 20% of declined buyers take a $300 downsell, the business recovers revenue and keeps a warm customer in the ecosystem.

## How to Apply

1. **Find the customer's next obstacle.** After buying, what will slow them down? Skill? Time? Accountability? Setup? Review? Access? The upsell solves that.

2. **Design the upsell around speed, ease, certainty, or status.** "We do it for you." "You get reviewed by an expert." "You get results in 7 days instead of 90." "You get the premium room."

3. **Build the downsell from the real objection.** If the objection is price, offer payment plan or smaller scope. If time, offer done-for-you setup. If uncertainty, offer diagnostic. If complexity, offer templates.

4. **Offer at the right moment.** Upsell after commitment, when the buyer understands the value. Downsell after a real no, not before you've sold the core offer.

5. **Track take rate and fulfillment cost.** A high-revenue upsell that crushes delivery may be worse than no upsell. The model must produce profit, not just gross revenue.

## Examples

**Situation:** A $2,000 course teaches service businesses to build an outbound system.

**Application:** Upsell: $1,500 done-with-you implementation week where the team builds the first prospect list, writes the first sequence, and reviews the first 100 sends. Downsell: $300 template pack and setup checklist for buyers who cannot buy the full course.

**Illustrative result:** Buyers who want speed take the upsell. Buyers who cannot afford the course still enter the ecosystem. Average order value and total buyer count both rise.

**Situation:** A SaaS product charges $99/month and struggles with activation.

**Application:** Upsell: $499 onboarding concierge that sets up the account and imports data. Downsell: a $49 setup template for customers who decline concierge.

**Illustrative result:** The concierge reduces churn among serious buyers, and the template gives price-sensitive buyers a structured path instead of leaving them confused.

## Anti-Patterns (tactical)

**Don't:** Make the core offer worse so the upsell looks necessary.
**Why:** Customers feel trapped when the thing they bought is intentionally incomplete. The upsell should enhance success, not ransom it.

**Don't:** Downsell to something that cannot produce a win.
**Why:** A cheap offer that fails creates a cheap refund and a permanent skeptic. The downsell still needs to solve a narrow problem.

**Don't:** Hide the real price behind endless upsells.
**Why:** If buyers feel tricked, the short-term AOV increase costs you trust, refunds, and reputation.

**Don't:** Ignore delivery cost.
**Why:** Upsells often add human labor. If you don't price that labor correctly, the "higher revenue" offer can produce lower profit.
