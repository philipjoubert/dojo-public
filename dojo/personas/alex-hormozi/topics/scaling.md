# More, Better, New: The Scaling Hierarchy

## When to Use
- You have something that's working and want to grow
- You're tempted to launch a new product, enter a new market, or try a new channel
- You've plateaued and can't figure out why
- You need a decision framework for what to focus on next

## Fails When
- You have nothing working yet; this framework is for scaling, not starting
- You do "more" of a deeply flawed process without fixing it first
- You ignore clear signals that your market has a ceiling
- You confuse "more" with "harder"; working more hours isn't scaling

## Core Concept

There's a hierarchy to scaling, and most entrepreneurs get the order wrong. They're at $30,000/month with a service that works, and instead of scaling to $100,000/month, they launch a course. They're getting 5 clients a month from cold email and instead of getting 20, they start a YouTube channel. They have one location doing well and they open a second before maxing out the first.

The hierarchy is: **More, Better, New.** In that order. Always.

**More** means do more of what's already working. If cold email gets you 5 clients, don't try a new channel. Send more cold emails. Go from 50 per day to 200 per day. If Facebook ads are generating a 3:1 return, don't try TikTok. Put more money into Facebook. If your sales team closes 20% at $5,000, don't build a new offer. Get more leads to your sales team.

The math: going from $30k to $60k by doubling what works is almost always easier than going from $30k to $60k by adding something new. Doubling what works requires execution. Adding something new requires learning, testing, failing, and iterating, on top of maintaining what you already have.

**Better** means optimize what you're already doing. After you've pushed "more" to its current limit (you're sending 200 emails a day and can't easily send 400), then optimize. Improve your subject lines. Refine your sales script. Upgrade your onboarding. Increase your retention. Get your close rate from 20% to 30%.

A 50% improvement in conversion rate has the same revenue impact as a 50% increase in leads. But it often costs nothing and takes less effort. If your funnel converts at 2%, getting it to 3% is a 50% revenue increase. That might come from one change: a better headline, a stronger guarantee, a faster follow-up.

The optimization order: 
1. Increase conversion rate (turn more leads into clients)
2. Increase average order value (charge more or upsell)
3. Increase purchase frequency (get clients to buy again)
4. Decrease churn (keep clients longer)
5. Increase lead volume (get more leads)

Most people start at #5. That's backwards. Fixing conversion first means every future lead is more valuable.

**New** means add new channels, new offers, or new markets. This is LAST. Not first. Only after you've exhausted "more" and "better" on your current setup should you add something new. Because "new" has the highest failure rate, the highest learning curve, and the highest opportunity cost.

Here's the real reason this order matters: focus. Every "new" thing splits your attention. If you're running cold email, paid ads, YouTube, a podcast, two product lines, and three market segments, you're running them all at 15% of your potential. But if you're running cold email at 100% of your potential, with a team, systems, and optimization, that one channel can become the whole growth engine.

**The revenue math of More vs New:**

Current: $50k/month from cold email (100 emails/day, 2% reply rate, 20% close rate, $5,000 ACV)

More path: 300 emails/day (same rates) = $150k/month. Time to achieve: 30 days. Cost: hire 2 SDRs at $4k/month each. Risk: low.

Better path (after more): Improve reply rate from 2% to 3% through A/B testing. $150k becomes $225k. Time: 60 days. Cost: $0. Risk: minimal.

New path (adding paid ads): $0 to maybe $30k/month. Time to achieve: 90-180 days. Cost: $5k-10k in testing. Risk: high. Could be $0 return.

The more/better path gets you to $225k/month in 90 days with low risk and low cost. The new path might get you an extra $30k in 180 days with high risk and high cost. The choice is obvious. But most entrepreneurs pick "new" because it's exciting. "More" and "better" feel boring. Boring makes money.

## How to Apply

1. **List everything that's currently generating revenue.** Every channel, every offer, every segment. Put the revenue number next to each. Rank by profitability, not just revenue.

2. **For each item, ask: "Could I do MORE of this?"** Can I send more outreach? Can I spend more on ads? Can I hire more salespeople? Can I get more referral partners? If yes, that's your next move. Not a new channel. More of this one.

3. **Calculate the ceiling of "more."** How many emails can you realistically send before deliverability tanks? How much can you spend on ads before returns diminish? How many salespeople can you onboard without management collapsing? That ceiling is your target. Hit it before moving to "better."

4. **Optimize with "better" once you hit the ceiling.** Now look at your conversion rates at each stage. Where's the biggest drop-off? If 1,000 leads per month but only 50 book calls, your call booking rate is the constraint. Fix that. If 50 book calls but only 5 close, your sales process is the constraint. Fix that. Always fix the biggest drop-off first.

5. **Only add "new" when "more" and "better" are exhausted.** When you've maxed volume on your current channels AND optimized conversion rates AND you still want more growth, THEN add a new channel. But add ONE new channel, not three. And use the same more/better/new hierarchy on the new channel.

6. **Audit quarterly.** Every 90 days, re-rank your channels. Kill anything that's not in the top 3 for ROI. Re-allocate that time and money to the top performers. Ruthlessly cut the bottom.

## Examples

**Situation:** A coaching business doing $40k/month from warm outreach (referrals and DMs). The founder wants to start a podcast and launch a course.

**Application:** More: How many DMs are you sending per day? "Maybe 5-10." That's your problem. You could easily do 50. And how many referral partners are actively sending you leads? "Two." Get 10. Before touching a podcast or course, max out warm outreach. 50 DMs/day + 10 referral partners could easily 3x the business.

**Illustrative result:** Instead of splitting focus across coaching, a podcast, and a course (and probably sucking at all three), the founder focuses on warm outreach. Revenue goes from $40k to $110k/month in 90 days. Now there's cash flow to hire a podcast producer and a course development team, so the "new" channels get proper attention when they launch.

**Situation:** An agency doing $200k/month from Facebook ads and cold email. Close rate is 18%.

**Application:** "More" is partially maxed (ad spend is at diminishing returns territory, cold email is at 500/day). Time for "better." The close rate of 18% means 82% of qualified leads are saying no. Moving close rate from 18% to 27% (50% improvement) would increase revenue from $200k to $300k/month. That's $1.2M/year in additional revenue from improving one metric. Solution: record all sales calls, identify the three most common objections, build specific handling for each, train the team, and role-play weekly.

**Illustrative result:** Close rate goes to 25% in 60 days. Revenue jumps to $278k/month. That's an additional $936k/year from "better" alone, with zero additional ad spend, zero additional outreach, and zero new channels.

## Anti-Patterns (tactical)

**Don't:** Launch a new product when your first one hasn't hit its potential.
**Why:** Your first product at $50k/month probably has a $200k/month ceiling if you just did more and better. But launching product #2 splits your focus, your team, and your resources. Now you have two products at $30k/month instead of one at $200k/month. And two products is twice the operational complexity.

**Don't:** Confuse "I'm bored" with "this is maxed out."
**Why:** Entrepreneurs get bored with what works and chase novelty. "More cold email" is boring. "Launch a YouTube channel" is exciting. But boring at $200k/month beats exciting at $40k/month. Your boredom is not a business signal. It's an emotional impulse. Hire someone to run the boring stuff and go find your excitement elsewhere.

**Don't:** Skip "better" and go straight from "more" to "new."
**Why:** Optimization is the highest-ROI activity in business because it costs almost nothing and affects every customer that flows through the system. A 10% improvement in close rate on 100 calls per month is 10 additional clients PER MONTH, forever. That compounds. Skipping optimization is leaving the easiest money on the table.

**Don't:** Do "more" without the infrastructure to support it.
**Why:** Sending 500 cold emails per day without a CRM, follow-up system, and sales process is chaos. "More" requires systems. More leads need more follow-up. More clients need more fulfillment. Scale the infrastructure alongside the volume or quality drops and you get a reputation problem.

**Don't:** Interpret "new" as "pivot."
**Why:** "New" means ADDING a channel or offer to complement what's working, not REPLACING what works. If cold email generates $100k/month and you want to add YouTube, keep the cold email running and ADD YouTube. Never turn off a profitable channel to test an unproven one.
