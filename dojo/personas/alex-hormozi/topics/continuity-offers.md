# Continuity Offers

## When to Use
- Revenue is lumpy because every sale is one-time
- Customers need ongoing maintenance, accountability, access, updates, or support
- You need higher lifetime value to make paid acquisition work
- You want customers to stay in the ecosystem after the main transformation

## Fails When
- The recurring value is vague
- The customer can get the same value once and leave
- Delivery requires endless custom work from you
- You treat continuity as a billing trick rather than a value promise

## Core Concept

A continuity offer is the recurring part of the money model. It keeps helping after the first transaction. The best version is not "pay me forever because I want predictable revenue." It is "keep paying because the problem keeps recurring, the environment keeps changing, or the value keeps compounding."

Continuity changes the math. A $1,000 one-time sale with $500 CAC leaves little room. Add $100/month continuity with 12-month average retention and lifetime value becomes $2,200. Same acquisition channel. Same customer. Different business.

There are several good continuity shapes:

**Maintenance.** The customer gets the result once, then needs to keep it. Fitness, bookkeeping, SEO, compliance, software, analytics.

**Accountability.** The customer knows what to do but needs pressure, review, cadence, or coaching to keep doing it.

**Access.** The customer pays for ongoing access to experts, community, data, templates, tools, or office hours.

**Updates.** The world changes and the customer needs current information: regulations, ad platforms, tactics, market data, playbooks.

**Consumption.** The customer uses the product repeatedly: subscription boxes, SaaS seats, memberships, credits, usage-based services.

The key is that the continuity offer should protect or extend the core outcome. If the core offer helps a customer get more leads, continuity might maintain the lead engine, review campaigns monthly, update scripts, and keep them accountable to activity. If the core offer helps them clean up books, continuity keeps the books clean.

## How to Apply

1. **Ask what breaks after the core outcome.** Do customers regress? Does the system need maintenance? Does the market change? Do they need review? Do they need accountability?

2. **Choose the recurring value type.** Maintenance, accountability, access, updates, or consumption. Pick the one that matches the customer's real ongoing need.

3. **Price for value and margin.** If continuity requires human time every month, price it like a service. If it is software, templates, or group access, price around perceived value and churn.

4. **Build the attach point.** Introduce continuity when the customer has felt the first win. "Now that the system is built, the next problem is keeping it working."

5. **Measure churn honestly.** If customers leave fast, the value is unclear, the onboarding is weak, or the offer isn't needed monthly. Fix that before adding more subscribers.

## Examples

**Situation:** A consultant sells a $6,000 project to build a sales process.

**Application:** Add $1,500/month continuity: weekly call review, script updates, objection-bank maintenance, and monthly rep training.

**Illustrative result:** The project creates the system. Continuity keeps the system alive and improving. The consultant's revenue becomes less dependent on finding new projects every month.

**Situation:** A creator sells a $997 course.

**Application:** Add $99/month implementation office hours, peer accountability, updated templates, and monthly challenge weeks.

**Illustrative result:** Buyers who need help implementing stay. The creator gets recurring revenue, and students get pressure and support after the course.

## Anti-Patterns (tactical)

**Don't:** Sell continuity when the customer has no ongoing need.
**Why:** They'll churn as soon as they notice nothing meaningful is happening.

**Don't:** Make continuity purely access to you.
**Why:** If every subscriber requires custom attention from the founder, the offer becomes a job with subscription billing. Build scalable pieces: group calls, templates, dashboards, community, reviews, or systems.

**Don't:** Hide behind annual billing to mask weak value.
**Why:** Annual prepay can help cash flow, but if the value is weak, renewals collapse and reputation suffers.

**Don't:** Ignore onboarding.
**Why:** Continuity churn often starts in the first week. If customers don't understand what to do, when value arrives, and how to use the offer, they leave.
