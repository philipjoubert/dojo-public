# Lead Magnets

## When to Use
- You need to build trust with prospects who don't know you yet
- You want to generate leads for your core offer
- You have expertise that, if demonstrated, would make selling easy
- You want to create a top-of-funnel asset that works 24/7

## Fails When
- The lead magnet is so generic it could come from anyone in your industry
- It's too broad; it tries to solve 10 problems poorly instead of 1 completely
- It requires 3 hours to consume and the prospect gives up
- There's no clear path from the lead magnet to the paid offer

## Core Concept

A lead magnet is a complete solution to a narrow problem. Let me repeat that because most people get it wrong: COMPLETE solution. NARROW problem.

Most lead magnets suck because they're the opposite: incomplete solutions to broad problems. "7 Tips for Better Marketing" is a garbage lead magnet. It's broad (marketing), incomplete (tips aren't solutions), and generic (everyone has these same 7 tips). Nobody reads it, nobody implements it, and nobody buys from you after.

Here's what a great lead magnet does: it solves one specific problem so well that the prospect thinks "if the free stuff is this good, the paid stuff must be incredible." That's the whole game. You're demonstrating competence by actually being competent.

Let me give you an example. Say you sell a $5,000 fitness coaching program. A bad lead magnet: "Free Guide to Getting Fit." A great lead magnet: "The Exact 7-Day Meal Plan for Losing Your First 5 Pounds." If you have verified client data, add it: "The Exact 7-Day Meal Plan That Helped [real number] Clients Lose Their First 5 Pounds." See the difference? The great one is narrow (7 days, first 5 pounds), complete (exact meal plan, not tips), specific without lying, and it solves a real problem (the hardest part of losing weight is starting).

When the person follows the meal plan and actually loses 3-5 pounds in a week, they think "holy shit, this person knows what they're doing." Now when you pitch the $5,000 program, you don't need to sell hard. They've already experienced your expertise firsthand.

**The "solve one problem completely" principle.** Every prospect has a chain of problems. A business owner who wants more revenue has sub-problems: they need more leads, they need to convert leads into clients, they need to retain clients, they need to raise prices. Your lead magnet solves ONE of those completely. If you solve the lead generation problem for free, they'll pay you to solve the conversion problem. If you solve the conversion problem for free, they'll pay you to solve the retention problem.

The key insight is this: solving one problem creates awareness of the next problem. The person who follows your meal plan and loses 5 pounds now realizes they need a workout plan, a supplement protocol, and a long-term nutrition strategy. They didn't know they needed those things until you solved the first thing. Your lead magnet creates the demand for your paid offer.

**The seven types of lead magnets:**

1. **Free trial or sample.** Give them a taste of the actual product. SaaS: 14-day free trial. Coaching: free strategy session. Course: free first module. This works because experiencing the product sells the product better than any copy can.

2. **Template or tool.** Give them something they can use immediately. Spreadsheet calculators, swipe files, scripts, checklists. These are high-value because they're immediately actionable and save time. A "cold email template that booked [real number] calls last month" is more valuable than a 50-page ebook only if the number is real. Without proof, say what the template does, not what you wish it did.

3. **Training or workshop.** Teach them how to solve a specific problem. Webinar, workshop, video tutorial. This demonstrates your teaching ability and expertise simultaneously. Record it once, distribute forever.

4. **Assessment or audit.** Diagnose their current situation. A free website audit, a business scorecard, a health assessment. This is powerful because the diagnosis creates urgency for the cure, which is your paid offer.

5. **Challenge.** A time-bound, action-oriented program. "5-Day Client Acquisition Challenge." This builds engagement and habit, and the completion of the challenge naturally leads to wanting the full program.

6. **Community access.** Free access to a group of peers. Facebook group, Slack channel, Discord. The community provides ongoing value and keeps your brand top-of-mind. But this only works if the community is active and moderated.

7. **Physical product.** Free + shipping offers. A physical book, a printed worksheet, a branded item. The physical nature increases perceived value and the shipping cost filters for serious prospects. This is why "$0 + shipping" book funnels work so well.

**The math of lead magnets.** Say your lead magnet converts 30% of landing page visitors into leads. You get 1,000 visitors per month. That's 300 leads. Of those, 10% engage with your nurture sequence. That's 30 warm leads. Of those, 20% buy your $3,000 offer. That's 6 clients, or $18,000/month. All from a lead magnet you created once.

Compare that to no lead magnet: you're asking cold traffic to buy a $3,000 offer directly. Conversion rate from cold traffic: maybe 0.5%. That's 5 clients from 1,000 visitors. Except each visitor costs $10 in ad spend, so $10,000 in ads for $15,000 in revenue. Slim margin.

With the lead magnet funnel, you can afford to spend MORE on ads because your conversion process is more efficient. You're spending $10,000 on ads to generate 300 leads that become $18,000 in revenue, plus you now have 300 email addresses for ongoing marketing that costs nothing.

## How to Apply

1. **Identify the #1 problem your target audience faces RIGHT BEFORE they'd need your paid offer.** Not a random problem. The immediate predecessor to the problem you solve for money. If you sell business coaching, the predecessor problem might be "I don't know where I'm losing money." If you sell fitness coaching, it might be "I don't know what to eat."

2. **Create a complete solution for that one problem.** Not tips. Not advice. A solution. Something they can implement today and see results. A meal plan they can follow. A calculator they can use. A template they can fill in. A process they can execute.

3. **Make it consumable in under 15 minutes.** The best lead magnets are short, specific, and immediately actionable. A 3-page PDF beats a 50-page ebook. A 10-minute video beats a 2-hour course. A 1-page checklist beats a 20-page guide. If they can't consume and implement it in one sitting, it's too long.

4. **Include a clear "next step."** At the end of the lead magnet, the natural next step should be your paid offer. "Now that you've [solved narrow problem], the next step is [bigger problem your paid offer solves]. Here's how I can help." This isn't a hard sell. It's a logical progression.

5. **Distribute through the Core Four.** Warm outreach: "I just made this free [thing], want me to send it?" Cold outreach: "I put together this [thing] that might help with [their specific problem]." Content: "I just released a free [thing], link in bio." Paid ads: "Get the free [thing] that helped [number] people achieve [result]."

6. **Measure and iterate.** Track: opt-in rate, consumption rate, engagement rate, conversion to paid offer. If opt-in rate is low, the headline or promise isn't compelling enough. If consumption is low, it's too long or too complicated. If conversion to paid is low, the bridge between free and paid isn't clear enough.

## Examples

**Situation:** An accounting firm wants to attract small business owners for their $500/month bookkeeping service.

**Application:** Create a "Tax Deduction Checklist for Small Businesses: 47 Write-Offs You're Probably Missing." It's narrow (tax deductions only), complete (all 47 with explanations), immediately actionable (they can check which ones they're missing), and it naturally leads to the paid offer ("Found deductions you're missing? We'll handle all of this for you every month so you never leave money on the table.")

**Illustrative result:** 40% opt-in rate because the promise is specific and the pain is real. 60% of opt-ins actually use the checklist and discover they're missing $5,000-$20,000 in deductions. 15% of those convert to the monthly service because they realize they need professional help. The lead magnet pays for itself from the first client.

**Situation:** A software development agency wants to land $50,000+ custom software projects.

**Application:** Offer a "Free Technical Audit: We'll Analyze Your Current System and Deliver a 10-Page Report on Performance, Security, and Scalability." This is type 4 (assessment/audit). It takes the agency 4 hours to complete but the perceived value is enormous. The audit reveals problems the prospect didn't know existed, creating urgency for the fix, which is a custom development project.

**Illustrative result:** 70% of businesses that receive the audit have a follow-up meeting. 30% of follow-up meetings convert to projects. The agency does 4 audits per month, converts 1-2 into $50,000+ projects. The 4 hours per audit is the best marketing investment they make.

## Anti-Patterns (tactical)

**Don't:** Gate a bunch of generic content as a "lead magnet."
**Why:** If someone gives you their email and gets a PDF of the same stuff they could find in a Google search, you've broken trust. They'll never open another email from you. The lead magnet must deliver genuine, specific value that makes them think you're an expert.

**Don't:** Create a lead magnet that solves the same problem as your paid offer.
**Why:** If your lead magnet solves the WHOLE problem, there's nothing left to sell. The lead magnet solves problem A. Your paid offer solves problem B, which they only discover after solving problem A. Different problems, not a free version of the same thing.

**Don't:** Make the lead magnet a mini version of your course.
**Why:** "Get module 1 free!" tells the prospect "the free stuff is watered down, so the paid stuff is probably watered down too." Instead, give away something different and complete. The lead magnet should be a standalone win, not a teaser.

**Don't:** Forget to follow up with the leads you generate.
**Why:** A lead magnet without a nurture sequence is like fishing without a hook. You got them to bite, now what? You need an automated email sequence that delivers additional value, builds trust, and presents your paid offer over the following 7-14 days. The lead magnet is the beginning of the relationship, not the whole thing.

**Don't:** Over-optimize the opt-in page and under-invest in the actual lead magnet.
**Why:** Getting a 50% opt-in rate means nothing if the lead magnet is garbage and nobody converts to your paid offer. I'd rather have a 20% opt-in rate where the leads are so impressed they become evangelists. Quality of the lead magnet matters more than quantity of opt-ins.
